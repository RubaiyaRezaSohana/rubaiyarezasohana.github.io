Thesis
Automated counting of blood cells using machine learning techniques

Blood cell counts are a cornerstone of routine diagnostics, yet manual microscopy remains slow and vulnerable to human error. This thesis develops an automated detection pipeline built on the YOLOv7 deep learning model to identify, count, and classify red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets directly from blood smear images. A custom-curated dataset, paired with targeted image preprocessing, feature extraction, and data augmentation, trains the model to recognize cell types reliably even under variable smear quality. The resulting system reaches approximately 97% accuracy — a faster, more consistent alternative to manual counting that points toward broader integration of machine learning in clinical hematology.

Thesis
Transfer learning-based CNN model for Alzheimer's disease classification, with Android assistance

Alzheimer's disease progressively erodes brain tissue, driving memory loss and cognitive decline that, without timely diagnosis and care, can prove fatal — making early, accurate staging from MRI scans essential. This project builds a transfer learning-based CNN, using VGG16 as its pre-trained backbone, to classify four stages of the disease: Non-Demented, Very Mild Demented, Mild Demented, and Moderate Demented. The model reaches 97% classification accuracy and is paired with a companion Android application that extends its reach beyond the lab — offering screening tests, note-taking, medicine reminders, object detection, and personalized health guidance for patients and caregivers.

R. R. Sohana, M. S. Ahmed, S. B. Shahid, and R. H. Hridoy, "Robust Blood Cell Detection and Counting Using Efficient Channel Attention-Integrated YOLOv10."
IEEE WIECON-ECE, Dhaka, Bangladesh, 2026, pp. 1090 – 1095
First author
Summary

Automated blood cell analysis plays a crucial role in early disease diagnosis, yet traditional microscopic examination remains labor-intensive and prone to subjective variability. Building on earlier YOLOv7-based work from her master's thesis, this study proposes an enhanced YOLOv10-based detection framework integrated with Efficient Channel Attention (ECA) to classify and count red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets from peripheral blood smear images, with ECA improving channel-wise feature selection without added computational overhead. An improved preprocessing pipeline using Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) further enhances local image contrast, refining the augmentation and feature-extraction approach used in the original thesis. The final model achieves approximately 97% accuracy across all classes, demonstrating strong robustness in both detection and categorization and highlighting the potential of attention-enhanced, lightweight architectures for reliable, rapid clinical hematological analysis.
